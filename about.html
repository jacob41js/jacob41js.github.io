About

Or, how this site came to be

Welcome. If you're new here, you may be wondering who put this site together, what this all is, and why it exists. And if you're future me, you may be wondering where in the world past me came up with this idea or how you've forgotten so many brilliant musings over the years.

This webpage idea came to me as a sort of trial run to explore the limits of AI back in 2025. In December 2024 I listened to an episode of Conversations with Tyler in which Tyler Cowen interviewed an expert on Stalinist Russia (Steven Kotkin, I believe). I don't remember much of the interview anymore, except the last rapid-fire section where Cowen asked his guest something along the lines of what part of Russia he liked the best, or, maybe, where would he spend time traveling in Russia if he had absolute freedom. Kotkin responded it would be Siberia, both because of the natural variety in the vast eastern tundra, and because of the pleasant nature of the people who lived there. I've had this dream of visiting Mongolia since at least 2012, spurred, in fact, by another podcast (an NBA one, of all things) I listened to where one of the guests mentioned off-hand that he was spending four weeks of the NBA off-season camping in a yurt in the Mongolian desert. That sounded amazing to me, for no obvious reasons other than I didn't think anyone else would really be there and the locale sounded so different than anywhere I'd ever been. Siberia, I reckoned in that moment, would probably slot nicely into that same category. Plus, I've had this interest in Russian literature since 12th grade when I momentarily convinced my AP English class to choose Anna Karenina as the classic novel we would study for one quarter of the year. (Surprisingly, everyone immediately gave up on this endeavor and I think we ended up reading The Sun Also Rises instead. Also, great book.)

Anyway, at the same time these various streams of consciousness were forming a confluence in my head, I was also thinking about Mortimer Adler's How to Read a Book. I had never tried reading deeply about a single subject. Even though I've been programmatically reading through the St. John's College curriculum since 2020, I would say my reading doesn't tend to follow a defined path. There are hundreds of books that are on my radar just because they are well-known as being “great”, plus there are those I hear about in podcasts or read about in other books, and in addition there's the dopamine rush of pulling out an eye-catching title at the bookstore or the library that have no relation to anything else in life. Adler's book, plus the talk of Russia and Siberia on the podcast, motivated me to give deeper dives a try.

Then, of course, there is what Aristotle would call the efficient cause — what actually brought this to fruition in the real (or digital) world: artificial intelligence. I had heard of people with no computer programming background being able to “vibe code” websites and apps using natural language prompts. It seemed intriguing, but I lacked any ideas for a good website. Then I remembered my oft-forgotten attempt at a commonplace book buried under a pile of notepads, broken plastic toys, and medical explanations of benefits, and I immediately knew what I wanted to build — a digital repository of my thoughts about all the books I've been reading that I can search on demand. It was something I've wanted for years, but never had the technological wherewithal to make it happen.

✦ ✦ ✦

So that's what this is — my ongoing attempt to catalog my thoughts about the books I'm reading. Since I was sent home to work during Covid in March 2020, I've been using what was previously my morning commute to make my way through books I never had time to read. At 5:30 each morning (give or take), I get up, make a cup of coffee, shuffle into my reading chair, and pick up where I left off. It's gotten to the point where my day feels incomplete if I do not perform these rituals, so I do my best to be punctual. This webpage is just a way for me to make that hobby more active. If AI is the efficient cause of this website, let me try to pin down the final cause. Someone once said, “Writing is thinking.” I do a pretty poor job of carving out real time to think, let alone write, between the hours of 6:30 am and 10:00 pm, so this small piece of the digital universe motivates me to do more of both.

I don't write, let alone post, at regular intervals. I prefer to let the muse (or an overzealous internal sense of guilt) guide me. At the very least I'm slowly filling out the Back Catalog page with mini capsules of books I've read. My deep dive pages (Siberia, Japan) I'll work on more deliberately. Everything else falls into a broad category, like Fiction, Philosophy, General Non-fiction, etc. The Reflections page has (or someday will have) pieces of writing that don't correspond directly to a specific book but are ideas I've been thinking about that often pull in books I've read — The Tower of BAIbel, for example.

So, Future Jake (or whoever has happened to stumble on this bespoke crevice of the internet), welcome. Hopefully Past Jake had something interesting to say that you're happy to be reminded of. And cheers to you if you've kept this little endeavor going for all this time.
